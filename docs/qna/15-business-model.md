# 15. Business Model

**What exactly are we selling?** — Enterprise risk-assurance SaaS.

**SaaS?** — Yes, our primary model.

**Enterprise license?** — Yes, for larger institutions with higher asset volumes and requirements.

**Risk assessment?** — It can be an initial paid entry product.

**Continuous monitoring?** — Yes, this should become a major recurring feature.

**Premium analytics?** — Yes, especially financial-risk and optimization analytics.

**Quantum optimization?** — It is part of the platform, not a separate "quantum fee."

**Regulatory intelligence?** — Included in the core platform, with advanced jurisdictions/frameworks potentially forming premium tiers.

**Implementation?** — Yes, especially for enterprise integrations.

**Managed service?** — Potentially later, but don't make services the core business.

**Which component is paid?** — The customer pays for the overall risk-assurance platform, not individual AI/quantum features.

**Who pays?** — Primarily the financial institution.

**How much?** — We should initially propose an enterprise annual subscription and validate pricing through pilots rather than claim a proven market price.

**Pricing unit?** — Best starting point: per institution + asset scale + feature tier.

**Per institution?** — Yes, this should be the main pricing basis.

**Per asset?** — Can be used for scaling tiers.

**Per user?** — Better kept secondary; enterprise value is not driven mainly by seats.

**Per regulatory framework?** — Could be a premium-tier factor, not the main pricing unit.

**Per transaction?** — No, that doesn't fit our core product.

**Annual subscription?** — Yes, this should be the core recurring revenue model.

**One-time implementation fee?** — Yes, for integrations and initial deployment.

**Gross margin?** — We should target high software gross margins, but we need actual infrastructure and support costs before claiming a number.

**Cloud/inference cost?** — Primarily cloud infrastructure, AI inference and data-processing costs.

**Deployment cost?** — Initially higher because of enterprise integrations; we should standardize deployment over time.

**Customer onboarding cost?** — Mainly integration, data mapping, configuration and security review.

**Annual contract value?** — We should validate this through customer discovery rather than inventing a number.

**Customer lifetime value?** — It should come from annual contract value × gross margin × expected retention.

**Customer acquisition cost?** — Initially likely high because enterprise financial sales are relationship-driven; this must be measured.

**How long to recover CAC?** — Our target should be within the first 12–24 months, but this is a hypothesis until we have actual sales data.

**What causes expansion revenue?** — More assets, more jurisdictions, more business units, continuous monitoring, advanced analytics and insurance/risk intelligence.
