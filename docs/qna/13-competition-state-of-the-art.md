# 13. Competition / State of the Art

**Who does asset discovery?** — Platforms like Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike, Microsoft and ServiceNow already have strong asset/security visibility capabilities.

**Who does attack-surface management?** — Companies such as Palo Alto Networks, Microsoft, Tenable and Rapid7 are established players.

**Who does GRC?** — ServiceNow, RSA Archer, MetricStream and OneTrust are strong enterprise GRC players.

**Who does regulatory intelligence?** — Thomson Reuters, Wolters Kluwer, IBM and regulatory-intelligence specialists already cover this space.

**Who does cyber-risk quantification?** — SAFE Security, Balbix, CyCognito, FAIR-based platforms and specialist CRQ vendors address parts of this problem.

**Who does cyber-insurance analytics?** — At-Bay and Coalition are examples of cyber-insurance companies combining insurance with cybersecurity/risk data; At-Bay is currently being acquired by Munich Re.

**Who does quantum-readiness?** — Deloitte, IBM, Qinsight, ISARA, Interlynk and others already work on cryptographic inventory and PQC readiness.

**Who does remediation optimization?** — Traditional GRC/security platforms prioritize remediation, while optimization itself is generally handled through classical risk/prioritization engines rather than our specific quantum formulation.

**Which enterprise platforms are competitors?** — The biggest competitive threat is actually the customer's existing stack, not one single company.

**Which startups are competitors?** — Specialist CRQ, attack-surface, crypto-posture and cyber-insurance startups are competitors for individual parts of our product.

**What do they do extremely well?** — They have mature integrations, large datasets, enterprise trust and years of customer deployment.

**Where are they stronger than us?** — Today, almost everywhere in individual product maturity.

**What gap do they leave?** — They generally solve individual layers rather than making financially optimized remediation decisions across those layers.

**Why can't a bank combine existing products?** — It can, and this is our biggest commercial challenge; KAIRON must prove that one integrated decision layer creates enough value to justify replacing manual cross-tool analysis.

**Why would a customer buy KAIRON instead of five tools?** — Because we are not trying to replace all five; we sit above them and turn their outputs into one capital-allocation decision.

**What is our wedge?** — Quantum-optimized risk remediation for financial institutions.

**What is our moat?** — The long-term moat is the asset → regulation → risk → financial exposure → remediation optimization graph, not simply the QAOA algorithm.

**What becomes harder to replicate?** — Our proprietary risk/decision data, financial-institution integrations, validated optimization models, historical remediation outcomes and customer-specific risk knowledge.

## Key positioning line to memorize

> "We don't claim to be 10x better at asset discovery or cybersecurity; our hypothesis is that we can make the remediation decision dramatically better by combining those signals into one financially constrained optimization problem, and we'll prove that through measurable cost, risk-reduction and decision-quality benchmarks."
