# 8. Financial Quantification

**How do we convert cyber/regulatory risk into money?** — We estimate the potential financial loss if a specific risk materializes.

**What is our financial-loss model?** — Probability of an incident × estimated financial impact = expected loss.

**What assumptions go into expected loss?** — Incident likelihood, asset criticality, downtime, data impact, regulatory exposure and estimated recovery/remediation costs.

**Do we calculate Annualized Loss Expectancy (ALE)?** — Yes, where sufficient assumptions and data are available.

**Do we include probability × impact?** — Yes, that is the basic foundation of our financial-risk calculation.

**How do we calculate business interruption?** — We estimate the financial impact of downtime based on the affected business service and assumed duration.

**How do we include regulatory exposure?** — We factor the potential financial impact of applicable regulatory requirements and control failures into the risk model.

**How do we include data-loss consequences?** — We consider the sensitivity, volume and business importance of the affected data.

**How do we estimate remediation costs?** — Each remediation action has an estimated implementation, technology and operational cost.

**How do we calculate residual risk?** — We recalculate the expected loss after applying the selected remediation actions.

**Can the customer see current exposure?** — Yes, they see the estimated current financial exposure.

**Can they see remediation cost?** — Yes, they can see the estimated cost of each recommended action.

**Can they see residual exposure?** — Yes, they can see the expected exposure after remediation.

**Can they see expected savings?** — Yes, we can show the estimated reduction in financial exposure from each remediation portfolio.

**Can we show ROI?** — Yes, we can compare the remediation investment against the estimated reduction in financial exposure.

**Can we show ₹10 Cr investment → ₹X Cr risk reduction?** — Yes, using clearly stated assumptions and synthetic data in the prototype.

**Why should a CFO care about KAIRON?** — Because KAIRON turns technical risk into a financial number and helps the CFO decide where limited capital should be spent.
