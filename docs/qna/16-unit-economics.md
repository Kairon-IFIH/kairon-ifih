# 16. Unit Economics

For the pitch, don't invent fake numbers. Use these as the working model.

**Average selling price?** — Start with a ₹25–50 lakh annual enterprise subscription hypothesis, then validate with pilots.

**Gross margin?** — Target 70–85% at scale, assuming the product remains primarily software rather than consulting.

**Implementation cost?** — Initially estimate separately and aim to reduce it through standardized integrations.

**Infrastructure cost?** — Cloud, storage, scanning, databases and quantum/AI compute.

**AI inference cost?** — Mainly regulatory analysis, summarization and agent workflows; we should control this with caching and deterministic processing where possible.

**Regulatory content maintenance?** — A recurring content/validation cost that should be built into the subscription economics.

**Support cost?** — Enterprise support plus technical/customer-success staff.

**Sales cost?** — Enterprise sales, pilots, partnerships and regulatory ecosystem engagement.

**Customer acquisition cost?** — Unknown today; we should explicitly call this an assumption until we sell pilots.

**Expected retention?** — We should target high retention because risk/compliance platforms become embedded in recurring workflows, but we need evidence.

**Renewal rate?** — Target >90% eventually, but this is a goal, not a proven number.

**Customer lifetime?** — Ideally 5+ years for an enterprise risk platform.

**Expansion revenue?** — More assets, jurisdictions, modules and insurance analytics.

**Break-even customers?** — Depends on team burn and ACV; calculate it once we finalize pricing and operating costs.

**Revenue at 10 institutions?** — At ₹40 lakh average ARR, ₹4 Cr ARR.

**Revenue at 100 institutions?** — At ₹40 lakh average ARR, ₹40 Cr ARR.

**Revenue at 1,000 institutions?** — At ₹40 lakh average ARR, ₹400 Cr ARR.

## "If I give you ₹1 crore today, how does that become ₹10 crore?"

> "We would use the ₹1 crore primarily to build the MVP, secure 3–5 GIFT design partners and convert those pilots into repeatable enterprise contracts; once the deployment becomes standardized, we scale the same SaaS model across more financial institutions rather than scaling headcount linearly."
